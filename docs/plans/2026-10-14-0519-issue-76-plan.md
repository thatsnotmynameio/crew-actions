## Planning Contract

### Key Technical Decisions

- KTD2. **The protocol has five calls in this part: `next`, `accept`, `update`, `log` and `done`.**
  - `next` returns a workflow's id, the version `AssignWorkflow` returned, its name and its resolved steps (names and commands), or nothing.
  - `accept` takes the workflow id and that version and returns the attempt id.
  - `update` takes the attempt id, a 1-based step position, the state (`running`, `finished` or `skipped`), the outcome, and an exit status or none.
  - `log` takes the attempt id, the position, a byte offset and at most 64 KiB of data, and returns the size the engine now holds (KTD7).
  - `done` takes the attempt id and the outcome.

  The runner maps the executor's 0-based index to the 1-based position, and its exit status -1 to none, in one adapter. The engine refuses a position outside the attempt's steps.
- KTD5. **The hand-out runs in one transaction, and only while the caller still waits.** `next` subscribes to the notifier (KTD6), then takes the oldest pending workflow and assigns it in one `InTx`. Before it assigns, it checks the request's context and the engine's stop. With no work it waits for a change, up to a 25-second window, and then returns nothing. The runner's call has no `http.Client` timeout, only a context deadline of the window plus a margin, so its client never gives up first.
- KTD8. **The runner's log writer never blocks, and a finished step's log is complete.** The writer `Started` returns appends to an in-memory buffer. A sender per step ships chunks of at most 64 KiB every 250 ms, or sooner when a chunk is full. `Finished` waits for that step's buffer to drain, then sends the `finished` update. So the `finished` state is the architecture's "no more" marker, and `log` carries no final flag. A runner-side reason (a step that could not start, a shell killed by a signal, a failed write) goes into the step's log as one line starting `crew-actions:` and to the runner's stderr. A workspace that cannot be set up is reported on stderr, and the attempt is done with failure.
- KTD17. **In this part the runner gives up on a failed call, except when idle.** An idle `next` retries every second on `ErrUnavailable`. A failed call during a workflow cancels the attempt, so the executor stops its steps, and the runner reports the error and asks for work again. SIGINT or SIGTERM cancels the runner's context, so the running step is stopped with its grace period and the runner exits without `done`. Part 5 replaces both (R17, R19).

## Implementation Units

### U9. The runner

**Goal:** the runner asks for work, runs one workflow at a time through the executor, and reports states, output and the outcome.

**Requirements:** R2, R21; KTD2, KTD8, KTD17.

**Dependencies:** U2, U5.

**Files:**
- Create `internal/runner/runner.go`, `internal/runner/observer.go`, `internal/runner/runner_test.go` and `internal/runner/observer_test.go`.

**Approach:**
- The loop does `next`, then `accept`, converts the resolved steps to a `spec.Workflow`, runs `executor.Run` with the backend and an observer, then calls `done`.
- A conflict at `accept` drops the workflow.
- The observer is the one adapter of KTD2's index and exit status mapping, and follows KTD8 for the log.
- A failed call cancels the attempt's context (KTD17).
- The executor's outcome maps to `model.Outcome`, and an error with no outcome follows KTD8 and KTD17.
- Any new `select` on a step's end and a context re-checks the end first.

**Patterns to follow:** `fakeBackend` and `recorder` in `internal/runner/executor/executor_test.go`; `docs/solutions/best-practices/select-on-exit-and-cancel-must-recheck-exit.md`.

**Execution note:** test the loop against an in-memory fake `protocol.Engine` and the executor's fake backend, then add one test with the local backend for real output.

**Test scenarios:**
- Covers AE7. Three steps where the second fails: the fake sees `running` and `finished` at positions 1 and 2, with exit status 1 for the second, `skipped` at 3, and `done` with failure, in that order.
- A step's output arrives through `log` with contiguous offsets, all before that step's `finished`.
- A step that prints 200 KiB, with a fake whose `log` is slow: every byte arrives, in chunks of at most 64 KiB, and the step still finishes.
- A step that cannot start sends `finished` with failure and no exit status, and its log holds one `crew-actions:` line.
- An `accept` conflict sends no update and goes back to `next`.
- A `log` answer that is not offset plus length cancels the attempt, and no `done` is sent.
- `next` failing with `ErrUnavailable` twice, then returning work: the runner retries and runs it.
- Cancelling the runner's context while a step runs stops the step and returns without `done`.

**Verification:** the scenarios pass under the race detector, and `internal/runner` imports neither `engine`, `scheduler` nor `store`.
